1Getting started

Prisme is a complete photo editor that works entirely on your phone: your photos never leave it, with no account and nothing sent over the Internet.

Edits are non-destructive: the original photo is never changed, every setting stays editable, and you save an edited copy whenever you want.

On first launch, Prisme offers a two-minute guided tour: it opens an example photo and lets you try the main tools, on the real screens. You can find it again at any time in Settings › Guided tour.

2The library

“Open a photo” shows Android’s photo picker: Prisme only sees the photos you choose there. One photo opens at once; several join the library.

“From files” opens raw photos (DNG) and photos kept in other folders. You can also share a photo to Prisme from the Gallery.

A long press on a photo starts a selection: touch other photos, then paste settings on them, apply a preset or export them all at once.

A photo’s menu renames it, duplicates it to try another version, or removes it from Prisme: the original photo stays in your gallery.

3The editor

At the top, the photo; at the bottom, the panel of the tool chosen in the bar: Presets, Crop, Healing, Masks, Light, Colour, Effects, Detail and History. A dot on a tool shows it has been changed.

Pinch the photo to zoom, drag it to move around.

  • Drag a slider to adjust: you see the effect at once.
  • Double-tap it to go back to its starting value.
  • Tap its value to type it.

The compare button, on the photo, shows a before / after divider to drag; held, it shows the original photo. The arrows at the top undo and redo.

4Presets and looks

Ready-made looks, sorted in families: Essentials, Cinema, Film, Film stocks, Black & white, Portrait and Landscape. Their thumbnails show your photo.

Touch a look to try it, touch it again to remove it; its amount goes from 0 to 200 %.

“My presets” keeps your favourite settings (without masks or crop), and imports Lightroom ones (.xmp, .dng, .lrtemplate or a .zip pack).

LUTs (.cube) can be imported too, with their amount.

5Crop and straighten

Drag the corners and edges of the frame, or the photo beneath. The format sets the proportions (free, original, square, 4:5, 3:2, 16:9…).

“Straighten” fixes a tilted horizon; “Rotate” turns by a quarter, “Flip H” and “Flip V” flip the photo.

Geometry corrects the vertical or horizontal perspective (leaning buildings) and lens distortion.

6Healing

Touch a flaw (dust, a spot, a wire): Prisme picks a replacement area and blends it into the photo. Zoom in for small details.

“Heal” blends the replacement area into the photo; “Clone” copies it as is, to repeat a pattern.

Drag the solid circle to move the healed area, the dotted circle to change the source; “New source” suggests another one. Size, feather and opacity are set for each correction.

7Light

“Auto” sets exposure, contrast and tones in one go; every slider can still be changed afterwards.

Exposure, Contrast, Highlights, Shadows, Whites and Blacks: enough to recover a bright sky or open up the shadows.

The tone curve refines the tones, for the whole photo or each channel (red, green, blue). Touch the curve to add a point, drag it into place, or out of the frame to remove it; on a small screen, set the finger sensitivity, or type the chosen point’s value.

8Colour

White balance is set automatically (the scene’s light, found in the photo), with the eyedropper on an area that should be grey or white, or as shot; Temperature and Tint refine it.

The “Colour cast” card tells which colour the photo leans towards, and its complement: it neutralizes it or halves it, if that cast is not intended.

Vibrance and Saturation dose the colours; “Black & white” converts the photo, with its own mix.

The mixer sets the hue, saturation and luminance of each colour; its eyedropper picks a precise colour on the photo (up to 4), with its range.

Colour grading tints the shadows, midtones, highlights or the whole photo, as in cinema.

9Masks: editing one area

A mask limits settings to a part of the photo. Prisme finds them on the phone, sending nothing:

  • the subject, the sky or the background, with one touch;
  • an object you touch (an animal, a building, a mountain, eyes…), at three sizes, with touches that add or remove;
  • the brush, the linear gradient (or its band), the radial gradient;
  • a colour or luminance range, picked on the photo.

A mask’s parts add, subtract or intersect; a mask can be inverted, soften its edges or blur its area. Each one has its settings: light, colour, effects, sharpness, noise, tint.

Pasted on another photo, subject, sky and object masks are found again there.

10Effects and frame

Texture, Clarity and Dehaze add depth or softness, and cut through haze.

The vignette darkens or lightens the edges (midpoint, roundness, feather, highlights); grain imitates film (amount, size, roughness).

Background blur keeps the subject sharp and blurs the rest, the more so the farther it is, like a wide-open lens.

The frame surrounds the photo: straight, rounded, oval, instant or square format, with an adjustable thickness and colour. The photo is never cropped: the export grows with the frame.

11Detail

Sharpening (amount, radius, detail, masking) enhances edges without exaggerating noise; masking keeps it to clear edges.

Noise reduction smooths the grain of low-light photos: luminance, preserved detail, colour. Zoom into the photo to judge up close.

12History and versions

Every setting adds a step to the history: touch a step to go back to that state; the later ones stay available as long as you change nothing.

Versions keep several interpretations of the photo (colour, black and white, crops…): switch between them, they stay with the photo.

13More options

The editor’s “…” button gathers: the histogram and clipping (blown areas in red, blocked ones in blue), shooting info (camera, lens, exposure), copying settings to paste them on other photos (all or some), creating a preset, saving a version, resetting everything, and reporting a problem.

14Export

The export button saves an edited copy to the Gallery, or shares it directly, as JPEG (with its quality) or PNG. From Android 14, a raw or HDR photo can also be exported as Ultra HDR: its highlights are brighter on compatible screens.

The size: the original one, or a longest side of your choice. Shooting info is kept or not, and the location can be removed.

In the free version, a short ad may follow some exports; the “Prisme without ads” subscription removes it.

15Settings, help and privacy

Settings (the cog in the library): language, vibrations, saving to the Gallery, the guided tour, and help: report a problem or suggest an idea (a pre-filled e-mail you review before sending), the privacy policy, the licences of the open-source components.

Prisme asks for no access to your photos, camera, microphone, contacts or location. The free version only uses the Internet for its ads and the subscription; your photos never leave the phone.

Privacy policy

Last updated: October 26, 2026

In short

Prisme edits your photos entirely on your phone. Your photos are never sent over the Internet, neither to us nor to anyone else. Prisme needs no account and collects no personal data for itself.

The free version shows a Google ad after some exports: for this, Google’s advertising service (AdMob) processes some device data, described below. The “Prisme without ads” subscription removes the ads.

Who is responsible

Prisme is published by prismedev. Contact: contact.prismedev@gmail.com.

Your photos

Prisme only accesses the photos you choose in Android’s photo picker, or send to it from another app. It keeps a copy in its private storage on the phone, with your edits and versions, until you delete it in Prisme or uninstall the app.

Exported photos are saved to the “Prisme” album of your Gallery, after you agree, or shared with the app you choose. All processing, including the automatic detection of the subject, sky, depth or the object you touch, happens on the phone: no photo and no information taken from your photos leaves the device.

Android permissions

Prisme asks for no access to your photos, camera, microphone, contacts or location. It uses Internet access, the network state and the advertising ID only for the ads of the free version and for the subscription. Google’s libraries add technical permissions, granted without asking at install, for their own operation: Google Play billing, background tasks and mobile network type.

What we receive

Nothing automatically. If you write to us with “Report a problem”, the message is prepared in your e-mail app and you send it yourself: we receive your e-mail address, your text and, if you leave them attached, the technical details shown before sending (phone model, Android and Prisme versions, graphics chip, last errors) and the preview of the open photo if you chose to attach it.

These messages are only used to answer you and to fix Prisme. They are neither sold nor passed on to anyone, and are kept only for the time needed to handle your request.

Prisme’s website (guide and privacy policy) is hosted by GitHub Pages, which may log visitors’ IP addresses for the security of its service; Prisme adds no analytics and no cookies to it.

Advertising (free version)

The free version shows an ad after some exports, provided by Google AdMob (Google Ireland Limited). To show and measure ads and to prevent fraud, Google’s kit collects and sends to Google the device’s advertising ID, the IP address (which gives an approximate location), information about the device and the app, and your interactions with the ads.

In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, Prisme asks for your consent at first launch, with Google’s form: you can refuse personalized ads and change your mind at any time in Settings › Ads: your choices. You can also reset or delete the advertising ID in Android’s settings (Privacy › Ads).

Google processes this data under its own rules: https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites

Subscription

The “Prisme without ads” subscription is sold and billed by Google Play. Prisme receives no payment information: only, from Google Play, whether your account is subscribed, to remove the ads. You manage and cancel it in Google Play (Payments & subscriptions).

Backup

If Android backup is on, Android may back up Prisme’s settings and your presets (not your photos) to your Google account, and transfer your library when you move to a new phone. These backups are handled by Google according to your Android settings.

Children

Prisme is not directed at children under 13.

Your rights

You have the right to access, correct and erase data about you (GDPR). As Prisme keeps no data about you, these rights concern the messages you sent us: write to us (contact.prismedev@gmail.com). For the data Google processes for advertising, use Google’s tools (myaccount.google.com) and Android’s settings.

You can also lodge a complaint with your data protection authority (in France, the CNIL: www.cnil.fr).

Changes

This policy will be updated if Prisme changes. Last updated: October 26, 2026.
